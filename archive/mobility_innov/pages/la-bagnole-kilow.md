## Snapshot

- **What it is:** open-bodied electric quadricycle in two homologations — L6e (45 km/h, AM permit, age 14+) and L7e (80 km/h, B1 licence, 16+); aluminium body on steel chassis, 650 L rear cargo bed, no doors or roof as standard.
- **Who runs it:** Kilow SAS, Haute-Savoie startup founded by Léo Choisel; industrialised by Groupe Savoy, a regional industrial group, in Cluses (Haute-Savoie, France).
- **Launched:** prototype presented at the 2022 Paris Motor Show; commercial launch and first deliveries 2024, with renewed public appearance at the 2024 Mondial de l'Auto.
- **Production:** Cluses, Haute-Savoie; vehicle and battery packs assembled in France — central marketing claim.
- **Price:** €10,800 base L6e (2026); doors +€1,290, roof +€490; L7e and dual-battery configurations push to ~€13,000+.
- **Performance:** 1× or 2× 5.6 kWh NCM packs, 70 km or 140 km range; curb weight 283 kg — exceptionally light for a quadricycle.
- **Volume:** pre-series and first-batch deliveries; target 1,000 units/year by 2026, mixed private + professional fleets.
- **Capital:** ~€10 million cumulative investment to date.
- **Status:** pilot production, low-volume commercial launch; first-batch ramp.

## What Makes It Interesting

- **Existence proof for non-OEM L6e in France.** First credible French startup to ship a complete L6e/L7e vehicle outside the Stellantis Kenitra platform — at 283 kg, lighter than every mass-market rival (Citroën Ami 471 kg, Microlino ~530 kg).
- **Inverted segment positioning.** The L6e narrative is dense-city commuting; La Bagnole targets rural leisure, country-home, and small-fleet professional use — a market Stellantis explicitly does not address.
- **"Made in France" as the entire product thesis.** €2,800 above Citroën Ami base, with doors and roof unbundled as paid options (+€1,780 combined) — the customer is paying explicitly for French industrial origin, not for feature parity.
- **Buggy form factor as engineering shortcut.** No doors, no roof, exposed structure — lets a low-capital startup ship homologation without the body-tooling investment an enclosed pod requires.
- **Groupe Savoy as industrial backbone.** Industrialisation runs through an established regional metalworking group, not a startup-built factory — the rare French micro-EV play with an existing industrial partner from launch.

## Current Landscape

- **Volume:** first-batch deliveries 2024–2025; effective output well under 1,000 units; the 1,000/year target is for 2026.
- **Pricing today:** €10,800 base (L6e, single battery, no doors, no roof, 2026); fully optioned L7e dual-battery near Toyota Aygo entry territory.
- **Direct competitive frame:** Citroën Ami (€7,990, volume incumbent), Fiat Topolino (€9,890, design sibling on same Kenitra line), Microlino (€17,000+, premium Swiss, struggling), Mobilize Duo (Renault, brand wind-down 2025), Toyota FT-Me (announced ~€9,000, expected 2026).
- **Known product gaps:** no regenerative braking at launch (promised in a 2025 software update); ergonomic awkwardness around indicator and light controls; doors and roof sold as options rather than included — early-product issues a legacy OEM would have closed pre-launch.
- **Distribution:** direct sales via kilow.com plus a small network of partner garages; no dealer infrastructure comparable to Citroën's ~1,500 French points of sale or Fnac Darty distribution.

## Pressure Points

- **Capital round:** €10 million industrialised a first batch but cannot fund a scale to mass-market volume; the next financing round determines whether output reaches the 1,000-units/year 2026 target or plateaus in the hundreds.
- **Price ceiling without subsidy:** the €900 bonus écologique for quadricycles was withdrawn Feb 2025 (see [[bonus-ecologique-quadricycle-removal]]); any reinstatement, or a harmonised EU L6e incentive under the 2027 review of Regulation 168/2013, would compress the gap to Stellantis pricing.
- **Toyota FT-Me launch:** if Toyota ships at ~€9,000 with ~2,800 European retail outlets in 2026, the design-and-origin pitch loses oxygen at the high end of the segment.
- **L-category regulatory shift:** any tightening of L6e safety parameters or upward revision of the L7e licence threshold under the 2027 Regulation 168/2013 review lands disproportionately on a low-volume producer with limited engineering buffer.
- **Industrial partnership:** Groupe Savoy's continued involvement, or strategic acquisition by a larger French industrial actor (Plastic Omnium, Forvia, an OEM partner), is a plausible inflection point — either consolidating the bet or ending it.
