BODY:

The page is **information-dense, bullet-driven, and fact-first**. Prose paragraphs only when a bullet list would lose meaning. Every bullet should carry a concrete fact: a date, a number, a named actor, a model, a regulation, a price.

**Total page length target: 250–400 words.** Scannable in 30 seconds, rewards a 2-minute read. If a section has nothing concrete to add, leave it empty or omit it — do not pad.

## Snapshot

The reference layer — 6 to 10 bullets, each a single fact. This block combines the at-a-glance summary and the hard data. Cover, in order:

- **What it is** — form factor, category, function, homologation (L6e, L7e, EDPM, etc.)
- **Who runs it** — operator entity, parent group, country
- **Launched** — date or stage
- **Production** — factory, location (e.g. "Stellantis Kenitra plant, Morocco")
- **Price** — base, key variants, currency, year
- **Performance** — range, top speed, battery, weight, charging — whichever apply
- **Volume / footprint** — units sold, fleet size, coverage area, with year
- **Status** — lifecycle position in one phrase

Name standards explicitly (L6e, L7e, EDPM, ZFE, AM, B1). Currency, units, and year on every number that ages.

## What Makes It Interesting

3 to 5 bullets — the editorial layer. Why does this entry exist in the wiki? Possible angles: unusual industrial choice, regulatory exploit, business-model inversion, design trick, political angle, counter-intuitive axis position. This is the section that makes the entry worth reading rather than just looking up.

## Current Landscape

3 to 5 bullets — state of play right now: latest volume/fleet figure with year, current pricing, direct competitors and relative position, recent events (last 12–24 months), substitution dynamics.

## Pressure Points

3 to 5 bullets — forces likely to move the entry over the next 1–3 years. Each bullet names a specific lever (regulatory, subsidy, competitive, capital, operational, political cycle) and what would have to happen for status to change.

WRITING RULES:

**Density over prose.** Bullets are the default. Prose only when a bullet would distort the fact. No throat-clearing, no transitions, no "it is worth noting".

**Facts before framing.** A date, a number, a named actor in every bullet wherever possible. Strip adjectives that don't carry information.

**Name things.** "Stellantis Kenitra plant" not "a Moroccan factory". "Anne Hidalgo's April 2023 referendum" not "a recent vote". "L6e quadricycle, AM permit, age 14+" not "a small electric vehicle".

**Numbers carry their date.** "65,000 units cumulative (2020–2025)" not "lots of units". "€7,990 base price (2026)" not "around €8,000".

**Currency, units, standards.** Always specify: €, km/h, kWh, kg, m. Always use the regulatory label (L6e, EDPM, ZFE, AM, B1).

**Link out.** Every page must have at least one Wikipedia link and one official-source link in the `links:` frontmatter. Use real URLs only — never invent.

**Editorial honesty.** If a figure is contested, say so in one clause. If a number is a target rather than a result, say "target". If something is announced but not shipped, say "concept" or "announced".

**Avoid:** hedging language, excessive caveats, marketing copy, generic descriptions, filler phrases ("it is worth noting that", "in many cases", "increasingly").

**Cross-references:** use [[slug]] format for all in-wiki links — these are resolved by the site.

**Length:** dense, not long. A good page is scannable in 30 seconds and rewards a 3-minute read. If a section has nothing concrete to put in it, leave it short — do not pad.
