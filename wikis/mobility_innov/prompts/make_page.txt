BODY:

The page is **information-dense, bullet-driven, and fact-first**. Prose paragraphs only when a bullet list would lose meaning. Every bullet should carry a concrete fact: a date, a number, a named actor, a model, a regulation, a price.

## Snapshot

A 4–6 bullet at-a-glance block. Each bullet ≤ 20 words. Cover:

- **What it is:** one-line definition (form factor, category, function)
- **Who makes/runs it:** named entity, parent group, country
- **Launched:** date or stage (concept, pilot, in-market)
- **Key numbers:** price, range, top speed, volume, fleet size — whatever the headline figures are
- **Where:** primary geography of deployment
- **Status:** lifecycle position in one phrase

## What It Is

Tight bullet list — 6 to 12 bullets — covering the concrete object or measure. For a vehicle: dimensions, motor power, battery, range, top speed, weight, homologation category, occupancy, charging, notable design choices. For a regulation: scope, trigger, effective date, penalties, exemptions. For a platform/service: vehicle type, app, fleet size, coverage area, pricing model, parking rules.

Each bullet is a single fact or tight cluster of facts. Name standards (L6e, L7e, EDPM, ZFE, AM permit, B1 licence) explicitly.

## Who Runs It

Bullet list — 4 to 8 bullets — naming the actors and their roles:

- **Parent / operator:** legal entity, country, ownership
- **Production / industrial partner:** factory, location, supply chain notable facts
- **Distribution:** dealer network, channel, direct-to-consumer
- **Capital:** funding rounds, total raised, investors, valuation if known
- **Regulator / counterpart:** ministry, city, agency that licenses or contracts the activity

Use named entities. "Stellantis Kenitra plant (Morocco)" not "a North African factory".

## Key Facts & Figures

A dense bullet block of dates, numbers, and milestones. This is the **reference layer** of the page — the thing a reader scans to extract hard data. Include:

- Launch dates, restyling dates, contract dates, ban dates
- Unit sales, fleet sizes, market shares, year-on-year changes
- Prices (base, options, variants) with currency and date
- Range, speed, battery capacity, weight, charging time
- Subsidies, fines, tariffs — amounts and applicability
- Comparative figures vs. direct competitors

Cite the year next to any figure that ages quickly. Round only when the source rounds.

## What Makes It Unique / Interesting

3 to 6 bullets — the editorial layer. Why does this entry exist in the wiki? What does it reveal that another entry in the same axis cell wouldn't? Possible angles:

- An unusual industrial choice (made-in-France, shared platform, atypical material)
- A regulatory exploit or first-mover position in a new category
- A business-model inversion (free vehicle, paid subscription, hidden subsidy)
- A design or engineering trick (symmetric body panels, doorless homologation, battery swap)
- A political or cultural angle (referendum, ban, cultural icon, generational shift)
- A counter-intuitive position on one of the axes — the cell it occupies that nobody else does

This is the section that makes the entry worth reading rather than just looking up.

## Current Landscape

Bullet list — 4 to 8 bullets — covering the state of play *right now*:

- Volume / fleet / utilization with the latest available year
- Pricing today, with variants
- Direct competitors and where this entry sits vs. them
- Recent events (last 12–24 months): launches, restyles, contracts, withdrawals, mergers
- Coexistence or substitution with adjacent services / vehicles

## Pressure Points

Bullet list of forces likely to move the entry over the next 1–3 years. Each bullet names a specific lever:

- **Regulatory:** named law, directive, category change, city decision
- **Subsidy / fiscal:** named scheme (bonus écologique, ZFE access, TVA, prime à la conversion)
- **Competitive:** named incoming entrant or merger
- **Capital:** financing round, IPO, acquisition risk
- **Operational:** safety data, accident rates, parking conflicts
- **Political cycle:** mayoral term, national election, EU mandate change

Each bullet should describe what would have to happen for the entry's status to change.

WRITING RULES:

**Density over prose.** Bullets are the default. Prose only when a bullet would distort the fact. No throat-clearing, no transitions, no "it is worth noting".

**Facts before framing.** A date, a number, a named actor in every bullet wherever possible. Strip adjectives that don't carry information.

**Name things.** "Stellantis Kenitra plant" not "a Moroccan factory". "Anne Hidalgo's April 2023 referendum" not "a recent vote". "L6e quadricycle, AM permit, age 14+" not "a small electric vehicle".

**Numbers carry their date.** "65,000 units cumulative (2020–2025)" not "lots of units". "€7,990 base price (2026)" not "around €8,000".

**Currency, units, standards.** Always specify: €, km/h, kWh, kg, m. Always use the regulatory label (L6e, EDPM, ZFE, AM, B1).

**Link out.** Every page should have at least one Wikipedia link and one official-source link in the `links:` frontmatter and the Sources section. Use real URLs only — never invent.

**Editorial honesty.** If a figure is contested, say so in one clause. If a number is a target rather than a result, say "target". If something is announced but not shipped, say "concept" or "announced".

**Avoid:** hedging language, excessive caveats, marketing copy, generic descriptions, filler phrases ("it is worth noting that", "in many cases", "increasingly").

**Cross-references:** use [[slug]] format for all in-wiki links — these are resolved by the site.

**Length:** dense, not long. A good page is scannable in 30 seconds and rewards a 3-minute read. If a section has nothing concrete to put in it, leave it short — do not pad.
