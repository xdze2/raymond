## What It Is

In October 2025, the City of Paris awarded a four-year contract for free-floating shared e-bikes to three operators: Voi (Swedish), Dott (Dutch, now merged with TIER), and Lime (American). Each operator is allowed to deploy between 3,000 and 6,000 bikes in baseline configuration, scaling up to 7,500 each during peak season — a combined fleet ceiling in the range of 20,000+ shared e-bikes for Paris intramuros.

The contract co-exists with the municipal Vélib' service (operated by Smovengo). Vélib' provides the dock-based backbone (mechanical and electric bikes from city stations); the three private operators provide the free-floating layer (electric bikes parked in painted zones, picked up via apps).

## Who Controls It

The contract is held by the Mairie de Paris and managed through the Direction de la Voirie et des Déplacements. The three private operators run the fleets, the apps, the rebalancing, the maintenance, and the customer relationship — under conditions, fleet caps, parking-zone obligations, and reporting requirements defined by the city.

This is a public-contract business model in the strict sense: the operator does not have a unilateral right to operate; the right is granted, time-bounded, and reviewable. It is the same structural model that Paris used for free-floating e-scooters between 2018 and 2023 — and which it terminated in [[paris-rental-escooter-ban-2023]].

## What It Reveals

The contract reveals that Paris does not oppose free-floating shared mobility as a category — it opposed the specific vehicle (the e-scooter) and the specific operational profile (chaotic parking, sidewalk riding, accidents). The same operators have been welcomed back with a different vehicle, under a tighter contract, into the same urban space.

It also reveals that the operator side has accepted a much more constrained settlement. Lime, Dott and Voi did not litigate the 2023 ban; they competed for the e-bike replacement contract two years later. The post-scooter European shared-mobility settlement is "operate at the pleasure of the city, on a four-year horizon, with the vehicle of the city's choice."

For Vélib', the contract is a structural complement, not a competitor: docked Vélib' remains the high-volume mass-market spine (76 million shared-bike trips in Paris in 2025) and the free-floating layer captures the more discretionary, last-mile use case.

## Current Landscape

**Stage:** active, in deployment from October 2025 through 2029.

**Operators:** Voi, Dott (TIER-merged), Lime.

**Fleet sizing:** 3,000–6,000 per operator baseline, up to 7,500 per operator at peak.

**Coexistence:** Vélib' (Smovengo) continues to run the docked mass-market system; the contract is additive.

**Data:** Fluctuo, the Paris-based mobility analytics company, is the de facto data reference for monitoring deployment, utilization and rebalancing across operators.

## Pressure Points

- **Mayoral cycle:** Anne Hidalgo's term ends in 2026. The contract is locked in for four years but a successor's posture toward extension or non-renewal will shape operator strategy from 2027 onwards.
- **Accident data:** if e-bike accident rates rise meaningfully, the political memory of the 2023 scooter ban will be reactivated. Operators have direct incentive to demonstrate safety performance.
- **Operator economics:** three operators in a single market means competitive pressure on pricing and unit economics. Free-floating e-bikes are more expensive to maintain than scooters were; the four-year horizon needs to clear a return on the bike fleet investment.
- **Subsidy / public funding:** the city contributes through public-space access rather than direct subsidy; any move to charge for parking spots or to require infrastructure contributions could swing the unit economics.
- **Vélib' boundary:** the docked-vs-free-floating boundary is currently stable, but if Vélib' adds more electric units, or if free-floating fleets are scaled up, the substitution pattern between the two services will reshape demand.
