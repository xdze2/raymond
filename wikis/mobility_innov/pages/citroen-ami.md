## Snapshot

- **What it is:** L6e two-seat electric quadricycle, 2.41 m long, 471 kg, 45 km/h cap; drivable from age 14 with AM permit (BSR) in France.
- **Who runs it:** Citroën, division of Stellantis N.V. (formed Jan 2021 from PSA + FCA).
- **Launched:** order opening Feb 2020, deliveries from June 2020 (France).
- **Production:** Stellantis Kenitra plant, Morocco — shared line with Fiat Topolino and Opel Rocks-e.
- **Price:** €7,990 base; Buggy €9,590; Buggy Palmeira €9,990; Cargo ~€8,400 HT (2026).
- **Performance:** 5.4 kWh battery, 6 kW motor, ~75 km WLTP, 3 h charge on a domestic 3-pin socket (no wallbox).
- **Volume:** 65,000+ units cumulative (2020–2025); ~13,000 France registrations 2024; ~45% of French L6e registrations 2022–2024.
- **Distribution:** Citroën dealers, direct online, and Fnac Darty consumer-electronics stores in France.
- **Status:** active, mature; volume leader of the modern French micro-EV segment, growth stalled post-subsidy.

## What Makes It Interesting

- **Repositioned a stagnant regulatory category.** Pre-2020, L6e in France meant Aixam/Ligier diesel "voitures sans permis" sold mainly to drivers stripped of their B-licence. Ami reframed L6e as a deliberate urban consumer product — electric, design-led, online-ordered.
- **Symmetric body panels.** Front and rear doors on each side are the same molded part, mirrored (front-hinged driver side, rear-hinged passenger side) — halves panel tooling cost and turned industrial frugality into the car's design signature.
- **Non-car distribution channel.** Selling a homologated vehicle through Fnac Darty and direct online flows bypasses the dealer-margin layer legacy autos defended for decades.
- **Platform leverage.** One Kenitra line, three brands (Ami, Topolino, Rocks-e); Ami is the volume anchor justifying the shared platform across Stellantis.
- **Subsidy elasticity, exposed.** H1 2025 sales fell −19% YoY immediately after the €900 bonus écologique for quadricycles was withdrawn (Feb 2025 decree) — one of the cleanest natural experiments on micro-EV price elasticity in the EU.

## Current Landscape

- **Volume position:** still segment leader in France 2025, but growth flat; H1 2025 −19% YoY.
- **Pricing today:** €7,990–€9,990 across trims (2026); Cargo priced separately for B2B last-mile.
- **Direct competitors:** Fiat Topolino (same Kenitra line, +€1,900, design-premium sibling); Microlino (Swiss premium, ~€17,000, struggling); Mobilize Duo (Renault, brand being wound down 2025); La Bagnole de Kilow (French rural play); Toyota FT-Me (announced Paris Mondial 2024, ~€9,000 target, expected 2026).
- **Recent events:** 2025 facelift (angular fascia, repositioned headlights, "capsule" badge); bonus écologique withdrawal Feb 2025; Buggy trim made permanent.
- **Adjacent substitution:** competes more with €3,000–€6,000 e-bikes and shared scooters than with B-licence small cars.

## Pressure Points

- **Subsidy reinstatement:** any return of the €900 quadricycle bonus, or a harmonized EU L6e incentive (informally floated in 2024 Green Deal mobility workstreams), would immediately re-accelerate volume.
- **Toyota FT-Me launch (2026):** Toyota's ~2,800 European retail outlets dwarf Ami distribution — first OEM threat with comparable scale.
- **ZFE expansion:** rollout of zones à faibles émissions under Loi Climat et Résilience 2021 (Paris, Lyon, Grenoble, others) mechanically grows the addressable market for licence-less electric urban vehicles.
- **EU L-category review:** any change to L6e parameters (45 km/h cap, 425 kg weight, occupancy) under the 2027 review of Regulation 168/2013 reshapes what the Ami is permitted to be.
- **AM-permit / minimum-age reform:** France raising the AM threshold above 14 would directly hit the teen segment that anchors the user base.
- **Stellantis strategy reset:** post-Tavares CEO transition (late 2024) — any reset of the Kenitra small-EV bet propagates across Ami, Topolino, Rocks-e simultaneously.
