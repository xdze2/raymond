## What It Is

The Ami is a two-seat L6e quadricycle: 2.41 m long, capped at 45 km/h, accessible from age 14 with the AM permit and from no licence at all in some configurations. It is symmetrical front-to-back — the same molded body panel is used for the front and rear doors, which open in opposite directions, an industrial-design trick that halves the part count. The drivetrain is a 6 kW motor and a 5.4 kWh battery for ~75 km of WLTP range. It charges from a household socket via a cable that pulls out of the passenger-side door.

It is sold as a car-shaped object that is not, regulatorily, a car. The targeting is teenagers and licence-less adults in dense cities; the pricing — €7,990 base — is closer to a high-end e-bike than to an entry car.

## Who Controls It

Stellantis (Citroën brand) designs and engineers the vehicle; production is at the Stellantis plant in Kenitra, Morocco, which it shares with the Fiat Topolino (essentially the same platform with Italian sheet metal) and the Opel Rocks-e. Distribution runs through Citroën dealers, the Fnac/Darty consumer-electronics channel in France, and a direct-to-consumer web flow that was novel for a legacy carmaker when it launched.

The Ami is a strategic asset for Stellantis well beyond unit volume: it is the OEM's proof that an L6e can be a brand vehicle, not a niche curiosity, and it underwrites the platform-sharing logic that produced the Topolino and Rocks-e.

## What It Reveals

The Ami is the existence proof that the L6e category — defined by EU regulation, not by market demand — can carry a mainstream consumer product. Until 2020, L6e was a category dominated by Aixam and Ligier diesel "voitures sans permis" sold mostly to people who had lost their licence. The Ami repositioned the category as a deliberate consumer choice for urban access, design, and price.

It also reveals the dependency of the segment on policy plumbing. The €900 bonus écologique was withdrawn in 2025 and Ami sales fell 19% in H1 2025 — direct, immediate, and visible. The vehicle is profitable enough to exist without the subsidy but not popular enough at full sticker to grow without it.

## Current Landscape

**Volume:** 65,000+ units sold worldwide cumulative since 2020. France remains the strongest market; Italy, Spain, and Germany are secondary.

**Pricing (2026):** From €7,990 for the base Ami, up to €9,590 for the Buggy (formerly limited, now permanent), up to €9,990 for the Buggy Palmeira top trim. A Cargo variant exists for professional last-mile use.

**Restyling:** A 2025 facelift gave it a more angular front and rear, new headlights at the base of the windshield, a "capsule" badge, and flanks visually echoing the 2CV. The facelift was a signal that Stellantis is committed to the platform long-term.

**Competitors:** Fiat Topolino (same factory, +€1,900, design-led, growing fastest), Microlino (premium niche, struggling), Mobilize Duo (collapsing, Renault retreating from the brand), Silence S04 (via Nissan distribution), La Bagnole de Kilow (French rural/buggy angle), and incoming Toyota FT-Me (the first true scale threat).

## Pressure Points

- **Subsidy policy:** the bonus écologique withdrawal is the single largest demand-side shock the platform has weathered. Any re-introduction of the bonus, or its replacement at the EU level (a harmonized L6e incentive has been discussed informally), would re-accelerate the category.
- **Toyota at scale:** Toyota's 2,800 European retail outlets dwarf Citroën's distribution footprint for the Ami. If the FT-Me launches at €9,000 and Toyota chooses to push it, the Ami's volume lead is contestable.
- **ZFE expansion:** low-emission-zone rollout across French cities mechanically grows the addressable market for licence-less electric urban vehicles.
- **Insurance and licence reform at EU level:** the 2024 EU insurance directive (≥25 km/h or ≥25 kg requires insurance) does not bite the Ami category but adjacent rule changes on minimum age or licence requirements would directly shift the user base.
- **Regulatory category competition:** the L6e definition is itself a political object. Any move to widen or narrow the category — speed, weight, occupancy — instantly changes what the Ami is allowed to be.
