## Snapshot

- **What it is:** two-seat L6e electric quadricycle, 2.41 m long, 45 km/h cap, no licence required from age 14 (AM permit).
- **Who makes it:** Citroën (Stellantis), built at Stellantis Kenitra, Morocco.
- **Launched:** February 2020, online order opening; deliveries from June 2020.
- **Key numbers:** €7,990 base (2026), 5.4 kWh battery, ~75 km WLTP range, 65,000+ units cumulative.
- **Where:** France (lead market), Italy, Spain, Germany, UK.
- **Status:** active, mature; volume leader of the modern French micro-EV segment, hit by 2025 subsidy withdrawal.

## What It Is

- L6e light quadricycle: max 425 kg unladen, max 6 kW continuous motor, max 45 km/h — EU homologation category.
- Two seats, 2.41 m long, 1.39 m wide, 1.52 m high; turning circle 7.20 m.
- Battery: 5.4 kWh lithium-ion; WLTP range ~75 km city cycle.
- Motor: 6 kW (8 hp) electric; 0–45 km/h in ~10 s.
- Charging: integrated 3-pin domestic socket cable stored in the passenger door; 3 h full charge on a household outlet, no wallbox required.
- Licence access: from age 14 with AM permit (BSR) in France; no licence required for B-licence holders; equivalent thresholds across EU member states.
- Doors open in opposite directions: front-hinged on driver side, rear-hinged on passenger side, using the **same molded body panel** front/rear — a symmetric-part industrial trick that halves tooling cost.
- Body: thermoplastic panels, no paint shop required in production.
- Variants (2026): Ami, Ami Buggy, Ami Buggy Palmeira, Ami Cargo (single-seat with 400 L cargo box for last-mile delivery).
- Not a car under EU type approval — no airbag, no ABS, no crash homologation as M1.

## Who Runs It

- **Brand / OEM:** Citroën, division of Stellantis N.V. (Netherlands-domiciled, formed Jan 2021 from PSA + FCA merger).
- **Production:** Stellantis Kenitra plant, Morocco — opened 2019, shared platform with Fiat Topolino and Opel Rocks-e (same vehicle, different sheet metal and trim).
- **Distribution:** Citroën dealer network; **Fnac Darty** consumer-electronics stores in France (novel channel for a homologated vehicle); direct-to-consumer online ordering from launch.
- **Capital:** funded internally by Stellantis (FY2024 revenue €157 bn); no standalone financing.
- **Regulatory framework:** EU Regulation 168/2013 (L-category vehicles); France — AM permit / BSR under Code de la route Art. R.211-2.
- **Adjacent operators:** Free2Move (Stellantis mobility arm) ran Ami carsharing pilots in Paris and Madrid 2020–2023.

## Key Facts & Figures

- **Reveal:** February 2020 (online order opening, France).
- **Deliveries start:** June 2020 (France).
- **Cumulative sales:** 65,000+ units worldwide (2020–2025).
- **France market share:** Ami captured ~45% of L6e quadricycle registrations in France 2022–2024 (vs. Aixam, Ligier, Microcar legacy diesel base).
- **2024 France registrations:** ~13,000 units (Ami line).
- **H1 2025 sales:** **−19% year-on-year**, directly following the withdrawal of the €900 bonus écologique for quadricycles (effective Feb 2025 decree).
- **Pricing (2026):** Ami €7,990; Ami Buggy €9,590; Ami Buggy Palmeira €9,990; Ami Cargo ~€8,400 HT.
- **Subsidy history:** bonus écologique for L6e quadricycles was €900 (2022–2024); withdrawn early 2025.
- **2025 facelift:** new angular front/rear fascia, repositioned headlights at the base of the windshield, "capsule" badge, 2CV-inspired flank styling.
- **Top speed:** 45 km/h (regulatory cap, not engineering cap).
- **Weight:** 471 kg (with battery).
- **Markets:** France, Italy, Spain, Germany, UK, Belgium, Portugal, Turkey, Morocco.

## What Makes It Unique / Interesting

- **Repositioned a stagnant regulatory category.** Before 2020, L6e in France meant Aixam/Ligier diesel "voitures sans permis" sold mostly to drivers stripped of their B-licence. The Ami reframed L6e as a deliberate urban consumer choice — design-led, electric, online-ordered.
- **Symmetric body panels.** Front and rear doors on each side are the same part, mirrored. The choice halves the panel-tooling bill and is visible to anyone who looks — a rare case where industrial frugality became a design language.
- **Non-car distribution channel.** Selling a homologated vehicle through Fnac Darty (a consumer-electronics retailer) and direct online flows bypassed the dealer-margin layer that legacy autos defended for decades.
- **Subsidy elasticity, exposed.** The −19% H1 2025 sales drop after the €900 bonus withdrawal is one of the cleanest natural experiments on micro-EV demand price elasticity in the EU — profitable at full sticker, not growable without the subsidy.
- **Platform leverage inside Stellantis.** One Kenitra line, three brands (Citroën Ami, Fiat Topolino, Opel Rocks-e) — the Ami is the volume anchor that justifies the shared platform across the group.

## Current Landscape

- **Volume position:** still the volume leader of the modern French micro-EV segment in 2025, though growth has stalled.
- **Pricing today:** €7,990–€9,990 across trims (2026); Cargo variant priced separately for B2B.
- **Direct competitors:**
  - **Fiat Topolino** — same Kenitra line, +€1,900 premium, design-led, fastest-growing sibling.
  - **Microlino** — Swiss premium niche (~€17,000), struggling commercially.
  - **Mobilize Duo** (Renault) — collapsing; Renault retreating from the Mobilize brand 2025.
  - **Silence S04** (Spain, distributed via Nissan) — niche European footprint.
  - **La Bagnole de Kilow** — French-made rural-flavored alternative.
  - **Toyota FT-Me** — announced 2025, the first OEM threat with comparable distribution scale.
- **Recent events:** 2025 facelift; 2025 bonus écologique withdrawal; Buggy trim made permanent; Toyota FT-Me unveiled at Paris Mondial de l'Auto 2024.
- **Adjacent substitution:** Ami competes more with e-bikes (€3,000–€6,000) and shared scooters than with B-licence small cars.

## Pressure Points

- **Subsidy reinstatement:** any return of the €900 quadricycle bonus, or a harmonized EU-level L6e incentive (informally discussed in 2024 EU Green Deal mobility workstreams), would immediately re-accelerate volume.
- **Toyota FT-Me launch:** Toyota's ~2,800 European retail outlets dwarf Citroën's Ami distribution. A €9,000 FT-Me with active push from Toyota directly contests the Ami's volume lead — expected 2026.
- **ZFE expansion:** rollout of zones à faibles émissions across French metros (Paris, Lyon, Grenoble, others under Loi Climat et Résilience 2021) mechanically grows the addressable market for licence-less electric urban vehicles.
- **EU L-category review:** any change to L6e parameters (speed cap, weight limit, occupancy) under the 2027 review of Regulation 168/2013 reshapes what the Ami is permitted to be.
- **Insurance / licence reform:** the 2024 EU insurance directive (mandatory cover ≥25 km/h or ≥25 kg) does not bite Ami specifically; minimum-age or AM-permit reform would directly hit the teen segment that anchors the user base.
- **Stellantis strategy review:** post-Tavares CEO transition (late 2024) — any reset of the Kenitra small-EV bet would propagate across Ami, Topolino, Rocks-e simultaneously.
