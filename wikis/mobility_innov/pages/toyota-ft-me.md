## What It Is

The FT-Me is a two-seater concept under 2.5 metres long, electric, fitted into the EU L6e quadricycle category. It was unveiled at Toyota's Brussels event in March 2025. The design lineage runs back to Toyota's mobility programs for the Tokyo 2021 and Paris 2024 Olympics, where Toyota deployed small electric pods for athlete and venue mobility — the FT-Me is the road-legal consumer derivative of that work.

Specifications are concept-stage rather than final: 45 km/h, target range around 100 km, target price under €10,000. A production version is announced as "expected within a few years" with no firm date.

## Who Controls It

Toyota Motor Europe. The vehicle is being developed by the New Mobility division, the same internal unit that ran the Olympic deployments. The head of that division stated publicly that "there is a market for a vehicle like this, and Toyota is able to produce it on a large scale at low costs" — language designed to telegraph industrial seriousness, not concept-car ambiguity.

If the FT-Me reaches production, it will be sold through Toyota's existing European retail network — approximately 2,800 outlets, dwarfing the distribution footprint of every current player in the L6e segment combined.

## What It Reveals

The FT-Me reveals that the L6e category has crossed the threshold from "regulatory curiosity exploited by Stellantis" to "consumer segment large enough to attract OEM-at-scale capital." Stellantis (Ami + Topolino + Rocks-e) had the segment essentially uncontested; Toyota's entry is the signal that other Japanese and Korean OEMs will follow if the FT-Me proves the unit economics.

It also reveals the strategic vulnerability of the current incumbents. The Ami's moat is a Moroccan factory and a five-year head start on consumer education. Toyota's moat would be distribution: 2,800 dealers immediately exposes the FT-Me to every Toyota service customer in Europe, a structural reach the Ami cannot match.

## Current Landscape

**Stage:** concept, with credible production intent. No homologation, no published final specifications, no announced launch date or final price.

**Strategic positioning:** explicitly aimed at the L6e segment dominated by the Citroën Ami; the public statements name Stellantis as the implicit benchmark.

**Distribution advantage:** Toyota's 2,800 European retail outlets vs. Citroën's dealer network plus Fnac/Darty plus direct web. The FT-Me would be in front of a much larger walk-in audience from day one.

**Industrial credibility:** Toyota is the only player among current Ami competitors that has both the scale to undercut Stellantis on cost and the brand trust to sell licence-less vehicles to wary mainstream consumers.

## Pressure Points

- **Production decision:** the gap between "concept unveiled" and "production confirmed" is the single most consequential decision for the segment. A Toyota production confirmation reshapes every business case in the table.
- **Pricing target:** "under €10,000" is a marketing line, not a commitment. The realized price determines whether the FT-Me undercuts the Ami or matches it.
- **Subsidy landscape:** the bonus écologique was withdrawn for L6e in 2025. The Toyota launch will land into whatever subsidy regime exists at that time; a re-introduction would accelerate, an absence would slow.
- **EU category definition:** any change to L6e speed, weight, or occupancy caps before launch would force a redesign.
- **Olympic-mobility heritage:** Toyota will likely lean on the Tokyo/Paris deployments as the credibility story. The marketing strength of "we already did this for the Olympics" is real and not available to competitors.
