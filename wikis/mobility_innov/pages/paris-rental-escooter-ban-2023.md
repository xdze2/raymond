## What It Is

On 2 April 2023, the City of Paris held a citizen consultation on the question of whether to renew the contracts of free-floating rental e-scooter operators in the city. The result, on a low turnout, was approximately 89% against renewal. Mayor Anne Hidalgo had publicly campaigned for the ban. The contracts with Lime, Dott and TIER were not renewed and from 1 September 2023 free-floating rental e-scooters were prohibited within Paris intramuros.

The ban applies to commercial free-floating rentals only. Privately owned e-scooters remain legal within the broader French EDPM rulebook (speed cap, age minimum, no sidewalk use).

## Who Controls It

The decision was the Mayor's office (Anne Hidalgo and her deputy in charge of transport, David Belliard), validated by the referendum and executed by the Direction de la Voirie et des Déplacements. The Préfecture de Police enforced the implementation. Operators — Lime, Dott, TIER — were notified and given the runway to summer 2023 to withdraw their fleets.

The political control of the decision is unusually clean: a Mayor with a public position, a binding-by-political-commitment consultation, an executable contract non-renewal. There is no national veto to a city refusing to renew a use of public space.

## What It Reveals

The ban revealed three things at once:

First, that free-floating shared scooters were politically negotiable at the city level. Operators had built business cases around the assumption that, once deployed, they were de facto incumbents. Paris demonstrated that a single municipal political cycle could remove them entirely.

Second, that the consolidation logic of the segment was incomplete. TIER and Dott merged in 2024 partly because the Paris withdrawal had removed a flagship European market and stripped revenue from both operators simultaneously. The ban accelerated industry concentration.

Third, that the regulatory category EDPM was not a stable settlement but a contested space. The French national framework permits private use; the city framework can prohibit commercial fleets. The same vehicle can be legal to own and illegal to rent in the same square.

## Current Landscape

**Status:** in force since 1 September 2023, ongoing.

**Vehicles withdrawn:** approximately 15,000 free-floating rental e-scooters left the city in summer 2023.

**Replacement segment:** Paris pivoted its shared-mobility strategy to e-bikes, awarding a four-year contract to Voi, Dott and Lime in October 2025 — the same operators, the same business model logic, a different vehicle category. See [[paris-shared-ebike-contract-2025]].

**European echo:** Madrid did not follow; Lyon, Marseille and other French cities did not follow at the same scale. The Paris ban is a singular event, not a national pattern — yet.

**Private use:** privately owned e-scooters remain legal in Paris and across France; the EU 2024 insurance directive (>25 km/h or >25 kg) now sits on top of the French national rule.

## Pressure Points

- **Mayoral cycle:** Anne Hidalgo's term ends in 2026. A successor with a different position on free-floating could in principle re-tender, although the political memory of the referendum will weigh heavily.
- **Operator strategy:** Lime, Dott and Voi adapted by pivoting to shared e-bikes rather than challenging the ban legally. The bikes contract is now the surface of their commercial relationship with Paris.
- **Accident data:** the Observatoire national de la micromobilité (created May 2024) reports a 58% rise in EDPM accidents in 2025, with serious accidents up 18%. The data, drawn mostly from privately owned vehicles since the rental ban, complicates the narrative that the ban solved a safety problem.
- **EU harmonization:** the absence of a unified EU EDPM rulebook means the Paris ban is locally robust but Europe-wide irrelevant; cross-border operators absorbed the loss and continued in other markets.
- **Replication risk:** any major European city running a comparable consultation could reproduce the result. Operators now write contracts and pricing with that risk priced in.
