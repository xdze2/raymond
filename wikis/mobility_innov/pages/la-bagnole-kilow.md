## What It Is

La Bagnole is a four-wheel electric quadricycle designed by Léo Choisel and produced by Groupe Savoy in Cluses, Haute-Savoie. It was officially launched at the 2024 Paris Motor Show. The form factor is a deliberately stripped buggy — exposed structure, open sides, a 650-litre rear cargo bed — visually closer to a Willys Jeep or a beach buggy than to a city pod.

Two homologation variants:

- **L6e** — 45 km/h, accessible from age 14 with the AM permit (or without for pre-2024 cohorts).
- **L7e** — 80 km/h, requires the B1 licence (16+).

Battery: 1× or 2× 5.6 kWh NCM packs, for 70 km or 140 km of range. Curb weight is 283 kg — exceptionally light by any car standard, achievable because of the aluminium body on a steel chassis and the absence of doors, roof, or comfort features as standard.

## Who Controls It

Kilow is a Haute-Savoie startup. Industrialization runs through Groupe Savoy, a regional industrial group, in Cluses. The entire vehicle — including the batteries — is built in France, which is the central marketing claim and a genuine differentiator against the Moroccan-built Stellantis platform and the Italian-built Microlino.

Total reported investment to date is around €10 million. The commercial scale is small: the announced target is 1,000 annual sales by 2026, a balanced mix of private buyers and professional fleets.

## What It Reveals

La Bagnole is the existence proof that a French industrial startup can produce a complete L6e vehicle outside the OEM platform game. It also reveals the cost of that proof: the base sticker is €10,800 — €2,800 above the Citroën Ami — and the vehicle ships without a roof or doors as standard (the door option is €1,290 and the roof is €490, totalling nearly €1,800 in essential additions). "Made in France" carries a price premium that the customer has to accept.

The product positioning is unusual for the segment: rural and periurban leisure, not urban mobility. The dominant L6e narrative is dense-city commuting; La Bagnole points at a country-home, leisure-property, and small-fleet professional market that Stellantis explicitly does not address.

## Current Landscape

**Stage:** pilot production, small commercial volumes; effectively a first-batch launch.

**Pricing:** €10,800 base (L6e, single battery, no doors, no roof). Doors +€1,290. Roof +€490. L7e and dual-battery versions push the configured price into Toyota Aygo territory.

**Known limitations:** no battery regeneration yet (promised in a 2025 software update), ergonomic awkwardness around indicator and light controls, and the door/roof being chargeable options rather than included. These are early-product issues that an OEM would have solved before launch.

**Distribution:** direct sales and a small network of partner garages. There is no dealer infrastructure comparable to Citroën's.

## Pressure Points

- **Capital:** €10 million is enough to industrialize a first batch but not to scale to mass-market volume. The next financing round determines whether La Bagnole reaches the 1,000-units-per-year target or stays at hundreds.
- **Price:** without a subsidy reinstatement and without volume-driven cost reduction, the €10,800-plus-options price ceiling tops out at design-led buyers and small professional fleets. The Stellantis pricing wall is structural.
- **OEM entry:** if the Toyota FT-Me launches at under €10,000 with Toyota's distribution, the design-and-origin pitch is harder to sustain.
- **Regulatory shift:** an upward revision of the L7e licence threshold, or a tightened L6e safety regime, would land disproportionately on a low-volume producer with little engineering buffer.
- **Industrial partnership:** Groupe Savoy's continued involvement, or a strategic acquisition by a larger French industrial actor, is a plausible inflection point.
